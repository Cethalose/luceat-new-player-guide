Luceat New Player's Guide

This is the durable player guide for learning Luceat. Its first available section is Character Choice Guidance: use it before `!create` or `!levelup` when you want to know what a choice means, what it changes mechanically, and how much complexity it adds to play.

Future guide domains can be added here later; this release is focused on character-building choices.

Start With The Kind Of Player You Want To Be

Luceat asks for choices in a practical order: name, ancestry, class, class skill proficiencies, required class feature choices, background, equipment, spell choices if your class needs them, then final review. Level-up choices appear later through `!levelup` or `!levelup [class]`.

Simple Front-Line Play

Choose Fighter, Barbarian, Paladin, Monk, or Ranger if you want clear combat actions and durable adventuring. Fighter is the most direct starting point; Paladin and Ranger add more spell and support decisions later.

Magic-First Play

Choose Wizard, Sorcerer, Cleric, Druid, Bard, or Warlock if you want spells to shape fights and exploration. These classes demand spell choices and reward planning.

Skill And Social Play

Choose Rogue, Bard, Ranger, Warlock, or a background with strong skill coverage when you want to solve scenes through scouting, knowledge, persuasion, stealth, or investigation.

Luceat is engine-authoritative: the game resolves mechanics, resources, combat targets, spell slots, proficiencies, and persistence. The narrator explains resolved results; it does not invent extra character powers.

Other Required Choices

Class paths, Warlock Pact Boon, Bard and Rogue Expertise-style choices, and spell configuration can appear during creation or level-up when the active class progression reaches that feature.

Invalid choices do not silently advance the character. Luceat keeps the prior state and asks for a valid supported option.

What Each Path Gives You

Artificer

  • Field Alchemist: support-focused specialist built around experimental elixirs. Choose it if you want utility and prepared solutions rather than direct weapon pressure.
  • Warding Smith: defensive craft specialist with ward and armor support. Choose it if you want an Artificer who protects the party and improves survivability.
  • Clockwork Sapper: tool and construct specialist with bounded helper support. Choose it if you want devices, practical utility, and a more technical playstyle.

Barbarian

  • Path of the Berserker: direct rage offense. It unlocks Frenzy, letting the barbarian push rage into a more aggressive assault pattern.
  • Iron Fury Path: reliable rage damage. Luceat records a bonus-action rage rider that spends 1 Rage and adds a deterministic +2 weapon-damage rider.
  • Storm Breaker Path: counterpressure and toughness. Its current supported features emphasize retaliation triggers and damage reduction.

Bard

  • College of Lore: broad knowledge and control. It grants extra skill breadth and Cutting Words-style interference with enemy rolls.
  • College of Masks: social infiltration. Its supported features emphasize disguise utility and flexible skill coverage.
  • College of Echoes: performance magic and support. Its features add inspiration riders and sound-themed spell support.

Cleric

  • Life Domain: healing specialist. Disciple of Life improves the vitality restored by healing magic.
  • Dawn Vigil Domain: protective support. Its Channel Divinity action spends 1 Channel Divinity to grant 6 temporary hit points as an ally-support action.
  • Ember Mercy Domain: restorative firekeeper. Its supported features combine healing boosts with fire-resistance support.
  • Stone Vow Domain: defensive anchor. Its supported features emphasize protective aura play and damage resistance.
  • Threshold Lantern Domain: sanctuary and vigilance. Its supported features emphasize sanctuary utility and undead-detection support.

Druid

  • Circle of the Land: spell endurance. Natural Recovery lets the druid recover some expended magic during a short rest.
  • Circle of the Thorn: controlling wilderness magic. Its supported features add alternate-form riders and terrain control.
  • Circle of the River: movement and recovery. Its supported features emphasize healing flow and movement utility.

Fighter

  • Champion: simplest offensive path. Improved Critical broadens critical hits to attack rolls of 19 or higher.
  • Tactical Dice Captain: resource-driven tactics. It adds Tactical Dice and a bonus-action Commanding Strike Die that spends 1 tactical die for a 1d8 weapon-damage rider.
  • Shield Line Veteran: party defender. Its supported features emphasize ally-protection reactions and guard stance.
  • Arcane Weapon Adept: martial magic hybrid. Its supported features channel spell effects through weapons with bounded spell riders.
  • Siege Breaker: forceful breaker. Its supported features emphasize object damage bonuses and push riders.

Monk

  • Way of the Open Hand: control through disciplined strikes. Open Hand Technique adds effects when using flurry techniques.
  • Way of Flowing Steps: mobility specialist. Its bonus-action technique spends 1 Ki for mobility and bounded bonus-action options.
  • Way of the Still Flame: mobile damage. Its supported features combine ki damage riders with mobility techniques.

Paladin

  • Oath of Devotion: classic sacred protector. It supports Sacred Weapon and Turn the Unholy for focused weapon empowerment and anti-fiend or anti-undead presence.
  • Oath of the Bright Guard: radiant striker. Bright Guard Smite spends 5 Lay on Hands resource for a 1d8 radiant smite-style rider.
  • Oath of the Open Road: mobile guardian. Its supported features emphasize travel aura and movement support.
  • Oath of Iron Mercy: damage mitigation. Its supported features emphasize ally damage reduction and resource-linked protection.
  • Oath of the Star Watch: radiant sentinel. Its supported features emphasize radiant aura play and fear-resistance support.

Ranger

  • Hunter: focused combat ranger. Hunter's Prey gives a supported combat technique against chosen prey.
  • Deepwood Stalker: stealth explorer. Its supported features emphasize stealth in terrain and tracking boosts.
  • Beast Bond Warden: companion ranger. Its supported features add bounded companion state and companion action support.
  • Gloom Trail Scout: dark-terrain ambusher. Its supported features emphasize initiative bonuses and dark-terrain support.
  • Storm Arrow Warden: ranged burst specialist. Its supported features add ranged damage riders and limited-use shots.

Rogue

  • Thief: practical movement and fast manipulation. It supports Fast Hands and Second-Story Work for quick object work, climbing, and jumping.
  • Knife Dance Agent: agile striker. Its supported features emphasize bonus-action attack setup and precision riders.
  • Shadow Ledger Spy: infiltration specialist. Its supported features emphasize social infiltration and tool expertise support.
  • Vault Runner: mobility specialist. Its supported features emphasize movement bonuses and climbing support.
  • Whisper Duelist: one-on-one disruption. Its supported features emphasize duel reactions and attack-penalty support.

Sorcerer

  • Draconic Bloodline: durable innate caster. Draconic Resilience gives draconic toughness and an armor-class base of 13.
  • Stormheart Origin: lightning and movement caster. Its supported features add lightning spell riders and movement bursts.
  • Starwrought Origin: radiant support caster. Its supported features emphasize radiant resistance and save-support aura play.
  • Deep Well Origin: resource-stable caster. Its supported features add a sorcery-resource buffer and limited spell recovery.
  • Ember Vein Origin: fire specialist. Its supported features add fire damage riders and heat-resistance support.

Warlock

  • The Fiend: aggressive patron. Dark One's Blessing grants temporary vitality after defeating a hostile creature.
  • Ashen Court Patron: fear and survival patron. Its supported features emphasize defeat-trigger buffers and fear support.
  • Mirror Veil Patron: illusion defender. Its supported features emphasize illusion reactions and attack disruption.
  • Deep Current Patron: cold and control patron. Its supported features add cold spell riders and forced-movement support.
  • Thorn Crown Patron: retaliation patron. Its supported features emphasize retaliation aura and charm-resistance support.
  • Pact of the Blade: weapon pact. Pact Weapon supports manifesting or binding a pact weapon.
  • Pact of the Chain: helper pact. Its supported features add bounded familiar-helper and companion-command support.
  • Pact of the Tome: spell utility pact. Its supported features add cantrip expansion and ritual utility support.

Wizard

  • School of Evocation: damage-magic specialist. It supports Evocation Savant for efficient evocation study and Sculpt Spells to protect allies from some evocation effects.
  • School of Warding: defensive spell specialist. Its supported features emphasize spell-shield reactions and ally protection.
  • School of Translation: language and ritual specialist. Its supported features emphasize language utility and ritual study support.
  • School of the Far Star: distant radiant caster. Its supported features emphasize range extension and radiant spell support.
  • School of Binding Lines: control and summoning boundary specialist. Its supported features emphasize summon-boundary support and longer control duration.

Multiclass Decisions

Use `!levelup [class]` when you want the next level to go into a specific class. Luceat keeps a class-level distribution on your character and prompts only for supported required configuration.

  • Starting attributes are currently broad enough to allow representative multiclass tests, but final eligibility is still governed by Luceat's level-up flow.
  • Wizard multiclass entry prompts spell configuration and persists spellbook, prepared spells, and slots.
  • Wizard level 2 prompts Arcane Tradition before additional spell progression configuration.
  • Bard, Ranger, and Rogue multiclass entry prompt one supported skill proficiency.
  • Previously completed starting-class choices should not be asked again during same-class level-up.

Unsupported Or Deferred Choices

This guide only describes choices Luceat currently exposes or stores. It intentionally leaves out options that may exist in tabletop play but are not supported by Luceat's current ruleset or prompts.

  • Unlisted ancestries, classes, subclasses, feats, spells, backgrounds, and equipment packages are not public player choices in this release.
  • High-level class features may appear in progression data before every downstream interaction has a complete player-facing workflow.
  • Some spell lists include entries above level 1 because they are implemented in the spell catalog, but character creation only asks for what the active spell-selection plan requires.
  • Use `!character`, `!abilities`, and `!spells` after creation or level-up to confirm your saved configuration.
